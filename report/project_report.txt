1. Introduction – Problem Statement
Autonomous navigation in desert and off-road environments presents a significant challenge due to visually similar terrain classes such as sand, dry grass, bushes, and ground clutter. Traditional computer vision techniques struggle to differentiate these categories at the pixel level.

To address this, we developed a deep learning–based multi-class semantic segmentation system capable of performing pixel-level terrain classification under synthetic desert simulation conditions.

Our objective:

Train a high-performing segmentation model

Optimize IoU performance

Ensure generalization to unseen desert environments

Deliver a reproducible and efficient inference pipeline

2. Methodology
2.1 Dataset
The dataset consists of:

RGB desert environment images

Pixel-level segmentation masks

10 terrain classes:

Trees

Lush Bushes

Dry Grass

Dry Bushes

Ground Clutter

Flowers

Logs

Rocks

Landscape

Sky

Train and Validation splits were used for supervised learning.

2.2 Model Architecture
We used a hybrid segmentation architecture:

Backbone: DINOv2 Vision Transformer (ViT-S/14)

Segmentation Head: ConvNeXt-style convolutional head

Patch size: 14 × 14

Resolution adjusted to multiples of 14

The backbone extracts high-level semantic features, and the convolutional head refines them into dense pixel predictions.

2.3 Optimization Strategy
To maximize IoU performance:

Backbone initially frozen, later partially unfrozen

Loss Function:

CrossEntropy Loss

Dice Loss (IoU-oriented optimization)

Mixed Precision (AMP) for faster GPU training

AdamW optimizer

Strong augmentations:

Random horizontal flips

Color jitter

Controlled resizing

3–4. Results & Performance Metrics
Final Validation Performance
Best Validation IoU: ~0.36–0.38

Pixel Accuracy: ~0.73

Dice Score: ~0.52

Training showed consistent convergence:

Loss decreased steadily

IoU improved across epochs

No major overfitting observed

Performance Analysis
Landscape and Sky achieved highest IoU

Vegetation classes showed moderate confusion

Boundary regions (Dry Grass vs Ground Clutter) were hardest to segment

Confusion Observations
Main confusion areas:

Dry Grass ↔ Dry Bushes

Ground Clutter ↔ Landscape

Vegetation overlap under lighting variations

This is expected due to visual similarity in desert scenes.

5–6. Challenges & Solutions
Challenge 1: Patch Size Constraints
DINOv2 requires input dimensions divisible by 14.

Solution:
Adjusted image resolution to fixed multiples (532 × 952).

Challenge 2: Class Imbalance
Certain classes like flowers or logs appeared less frequently.

Solution:

Dice Loss added to improve minority class segmentation

Strong data augmentation applied

Challenge 3: GPU Utilization & Stability
Initial runs showed inconsistent GPU usage and high training time.

Solution:

Mixed precision (AMP)

Backbone freezing during early training

Efficient batch size tuning

Challenge 4: Domain Generalization
Validation images were from unseen desert twins.

Solution:

Robust feature extractor (DINOv2)

Augmentation strategies

Careful validation monitoring

7. Conclusion
